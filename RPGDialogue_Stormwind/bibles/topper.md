# Topper McNabb (Trade District beggar)

**Canon:** a human beggar in Stormwind's Trade District who shouts for alms and pretends to be blind.
**Voice:** cheerful, theatrical, self-mocking; an old farmer's idiom ("friend", pumpkins, furrows, carthorses). Whole, warm run-on sentences. A tall-tale teller who changes the story for whoever is paying, and goes quiet and plain only when the truth comes out.
**Tier:** major (1.26.0, the Topper McNabb patch). File: `npcs/topper.lua` (moved out of `city3.lua`; key `topper` kept).

## Authored, not canon
- Born on a farm near Goldshire; it burned in the First War (he ran with a pitchfork; his father stood at the gate with a scythe and died). After the Second War, a tenant farm of forty acres south of Sentinel Hill on the Moonbrook road, under a noble seal he never saw. A pumpkin ribboned at the Goldshire fair.
- The truth (Persuade): rent up, rain gone, the Defias asked for a share, he refused, they burned the barn, and he drove off before dawn with his wife and a sack of seed potatoes, gate left open, no goodbyes to the Saldeans or Furlbrows.
- Wife **Elsie McNabb**, alive, in Lakeshire with her sister for four years; she said she'd wait until he was a farmer again. He writes a letter every Winter Veil and never posts any (six, all saying he's doing well).
- Nine toes (a Goldshire carthorse, outside the Lion's Pride, the day he courted Elsie). Age fifty-six; six years on the street.
- The "blind man's miracle" act; a Cathedral priest's real blessing that sharpened his eyes.
- Sleeps under a canal arch with a one-eared orange cat, **the Duchess**. Allison of the Gilded Rose leaves the burnt loaf ends out for him. A guard sergeant pays for armour shining in pie. Shines armour with goose fat.
- Orphans steal his hat on Mondays and return it with something in it. A Gnomeregan refugee built him a stove that exploded.
- Hauled stone for the masons; a quiet mason he knew was at Moonbrook in a mask and looked away.
- Saw a fine-cloaked gentleman (grey temples, old books and lavender, rings) paying a man in black by the canal after midnight, talking of Westfall. Never named: dramatic irony for Lescovar, nothing more.
- Trias's cheese shop has visitors who don't buy cheese (dramatic irony for SI:7).
- Met King Llane at a harvest fair as a boy; Llane bought a turnip.
- Small tall tales: the kobold and the lantern, the murloc that followed him home, a Riverpaw gnoll in his second-best hat, the tram fish.
